03 — SURVIVAL GUIDE

TIPS +
TRICKS

Things you'll probably figure out after making the mistake once.

01

Academic tips

1. Go to class, especially during the first few weeks. It’s much easier to stay caught up than to recover after falling behind. 2. Check Canvas every day. Assignments, announcements, quizzes, and deadline changes can sneak up on you. 3. Put every deadline in your calendar. Don’t rely on remembering it from class. 4. Read the syllabus early and save i. Look for attendance policies, dropped assignments, exam dates, grading policies, and office hours. 5. Use office hours BEFORE you’re completely lost. Professors are much easier to approach when you have a specific question. 6. Don’t wait until exam week to study. College material piles up quickly. 7. Use UC academic support. Tutoring, MASS, SRS, the Academic Writing Center, and academic coaching exist for a reason. 8. One bad grade isn't the end of the semester. Figure out what went wrong and adjust early. 9. Learn your route and build your backpack

02

Campus survival

1. Learn the important buildings during your first week. Know where your classes, dining locations, library, health services, and residence hall are. 2. Leave earlier than you think you need to. Your first few walks across campus will take longer than Google Maps suggests. 3. Take screenshots of your class schedule. Your phone dying shouldn't leave you wandering around campus. 4. Learn the shuttle system. It can save you a LOT of walking, especially when the weather is terrible. 5. Carry an umbrella. Cincinnati weather can change ridiculously fast. 6. Have a backup route to class. Construction, events, weather, or crowds can mess with your normal route. 7. Don't be embarrassed to look at a campus map. Everyone was lost at some point.

03

Food & money

1. Don't spend your Bearcat Cash/food money just because you have it. Budget it across the semester. 2. Figure out which dining locations you actually like early. 3. Keep easy food in your room. There will be nights when you absolutely do not want to leave your dorm. 4. Don't buy everything during move-in. You will discover what you actually need after living there for a few weeks. 5. Watch the little purchases. $6 here + $8 there + random Starbucks = surprisingly expensive.

04

Dorm life

1. You don't need to bring your entire bedroom from home. 2. Bring a small toolkit. Scissors, tape, screwdriver, command strips, etc. can save you. 3. Label important belongings. 4. Keep basic medicine and first-aid supplies available. 5. Do your laundry before you run out of clean clothes. Future-you will appreciate it. 6. Talk to your roommates about expectations early. Cleaning, guests, noise, food, and shared items are much easier to discuss before they become arguments. 7. Your room doesn't have to look perfect. You're living there, not filming a dorm tour.

05

Making friends

1. Don't expect your first friends to necessarily become your best friends. 2. Say yes to things during the first few weeks. Events are much easier places to meet people than randomly approaching strangers. 3. Join something you genuinely like. You don't need 15 clubs. 4. Go to events alone if you have to. A surprising number of people are also there alone. 5. Ask simple questions: “What's your major?”, “Are you a freshman too?”, “Have you been to this event before?” 6. Follow up. “Want to grab lunch sometime?” is how acquaintances become friends. 7. Don't panic if everyone seems to have a friend group already. A lot of those groups are temporary first-week groups.

06

Career Tips

Start building your resume and LinkedIn early. Go to career fairs even if you aren't looking for a job yet. Build projects and keep track of what you accomplish. Talk to professors, upperclassmen, alumni, and recruiters. Learn skills outside your classes. Don't feel pressured to get an internship your first year.

07

International Student Tips

1. Know your visa and work restrictions. 2. Keep important immigration documents organized. 3. Ask your international student office before making decisions that could affect your status. 4. Learn basic U.S. banking, taxes, healthcare, and workplace norms. 5. Don't be afraid to ask when something works differently than you're used to.

08

Commuter Student Tips

1. Sign up for transit and shuttle services. Make sure you have your pass in time for classes, otherwise contact transportation services. 2. Try not to miss your shuttle because they arrive every thirty minutes. 3. Account for traffic during rush hours. 4. Stay on campus between classes when you can. 5. Join clubs or organizations like commuter student lounge to meet people. 6. Find a few reliable places to study. 7. Keep an emergency charger, snacks, and other essentials with you. 8. Have a backup transportation plan. 9. Don't feel like you're missing out just because you don't live on campus.

09

Take A Break

College can make it feel like you should always be doing something. You don't. Eat properly, get enough sleep, take breaks, and give yourself time to reset. Sometimes going back to your room, putting your phone down, and doing absolutely nothing for a while is exactly what you need.
